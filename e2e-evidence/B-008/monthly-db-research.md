## Source-based need and narrow supported operation

For an active monthly lease covering a complete requested calendar month, use its stored monthly rent and currency exactly once, with an explicit rent line carrying the month as a half-open `service_period`. Due day 31 is the last day of that month (base schema comment); 29/30 without that date in a shorter month is unresolved unless an authoritative fallback is found. Deposits and utilities are not monthly rent. No partial-month, quarterly/yearly, unknown historical coverage, automatic void/reissue or extra-fee formula is authorized here. Preview and eventual generation must use the same calculation and a freshly rechecked complete source snapshot.

Inspected base `20261018120000_schema.sql`: `invoices` is unique on `(org_id,id)` and `(org_id,invoice_number)`, `invoice_lines` on `(invoice_id,line_no)` and allocated utility line; no rent-period invariant. `20261028130000_utility_charge_posting.sql` supplies `(org_id,id)` on invoice lines. `20261031100211_b001_esign_activation.sql` protects one live **move-in** invoice, not recurring rent. Search across later migration definitions found B010/B017 notification occurrence uniqueness keyed to existing invoice, not an invoice-generation identity. Reinspect actual live catalog and all branches: source inspection is not a deployed privilege/catalog fingerprint.

`src/server/move-in-invoice.ts` creates a move-in invoice and rent line; its domain callers lock the lease, but historical lines may lack `service_period`. `src/server/utility-posting.ts` independently locks and appends to existing invoice rows or creates utility invoices. `src/server/seed.ts` is a disposable fixture writer. Base team RLS allows admin/manager/accountant invoice/line DML and broad authenticated/service table DML grants, subject to RLS and later changes; ordinary direct inserts can skip a new cooperative lease lock. Locking the lease would serialize cooperating new writers, not an independent old/direct line insertion. An application-only lease lock is viable **only** if the enforceable product contract actually restricts every relevant writer to that protocol; that restriction is not established by the inspected source. A durable occurrence key is warranted for concurrent new requests; all-writer overlap protection is a separate requirement, not solved by the key alone.

## Reference foundation and its explicit limitations

The illustrative owner-only occurrence table stores one `(org,lease,month_start)` and the resulting actual invoice and line IDs. The command table stores an org/actor/request identity and a 32-byte canonical request digest, mapping retries to the occurrence. Composite FKs bind each object to the org. `invoice_lines(org_id,id)` depends on the cited migration. Neither table contains raw tenant contact data, payment data or arbitrary request bodies. RLS is enabled, no policies or app/PUBLIC grants are given, and FK deletes are RESTRICT; owner privilege remains trusted. No privileged callable exists. Verify current app schema/default privileges, function and trigger execution rights, and table owners on PE; these statements do not prove a deployed ACL.

**Important missing invariants:** two independent composite FKs do not prove that the line belongs to the chosen invoice/lease, is rent, has the exact intended service period/currency/amount or is financially posted. The reference has no same-transaction identity/immutability guard, source lifecycle transition, old-writer fence, final actor authorization, receipt, ledger validation or safe concurrent gateway. An owner can change it. The table's uniqueness cannot prevent an old/direct invoice line with overlapping rent or an existing unbound move-in row. It must not be marketed as global duplicate prevention. A command row maps a completed atomic result, not an in-flight task or evidence that an uncertain COMMIT failed. Store command, occurrence, invoice, line and balanced ledger atomically within one reviewed operation; handle uncertain acknowledgment through the same org/actor/request+digest and source identity, never mint a replacement request.

A subsequent proposal must decide exactly how to protect the actual invoice line/source identity and status: narrowly scoped reviewed trigger/privilege boundary covering every relevant writer, or a proven single fixed gateway with no bypass. Do not add an all-invoice trigger or broadly revoke legacy writes on the basis of this draft. No executable gateway or role grants are specified because the writer, legacy fence and lock protocol have not been reviewed. If direct clients remain authorized and bypass it, restrict claims to cooperating new writers; do not release a global uniqueness guarantee.
