The current local implementation marks transfer headers `source_table='b014_owner_transfer_v1'` and `source_id IS NULL`, with an `owner_payout` or reversal and exactly two opposite `owner_payable`/`cash_clearing` entries. Request/payload metadata is being revised; this draft does not freeze its encoding. The current application and its `closeMonth` take the same validated per-organization transaction advisory lock `condola:owner-transfer-month:<lowercase-org-UUID>`. This coordinates participating current code only. Existing generic reversal, direct ledger inserts and older close workers do not necessarily participate, so the final validation-to-commit race is unresolved. Manual recorded transfer is a claim in the application, not proof of bank settlement or a platform remittance.

## Current source and practical boundary

`supabase/migrations/20261018120000_schema.sql:888–936,1561–1573,1617–1634,2069–2070,2385–2387` defines the ledger, one-reversal uniqueness, append-only update/delete rules, balance check and authenticated UPDATE/DELETE revocation. A balanced later insert may still add entries to an existing transaction; balanced does not mean exactly two or sealed. `20261030120000_org_scoped_reversal_audit.sql:18–22,34–112` provides both executable authenticated/service-role generic `post_reversal` overloads; each copies the original source and its entries. `20261020120000_auth.sql:53–60,76–86` permits authorized month-close inserts. Current `src/server/owner-transfers.ts` creates/reverses typed records with direct inserts and validates their shape; `src/server/owner-transfer-period-lock.ts` defines the cooperative lock and `src/server/domain.ts:2653–2668` uses it in current close. Existing owner RLS covers issued statements, not a purpose-built typed transfer projection (`20261018120000_schema.sql:2213–2222`).

A narrow future boundary could use existing ledger records without a new table if the exact role, privilege and migration analysis supports it:

1. Codex designs fixed, narrowly parameterized create/replay and reversal gateways. Each must derive and verify actor/session, org and live/sandbox relationship, current staff authority, global/org OFF and allowlist controls, effective property/owner relationship, currency, source identity, amount and date. Under the common org transaction lock it must check closed periods, compare request and canonical payload on retry, insert exactly one typed header and the two prescribed mirrored legs atomically, and recheck exact shape, original reversal linkage and current authorization before commit. Changed payload for the same request rejects; an exact retry returns its original result. Reversal never copies arbitrary newly appended entries. The database's one-reversal unique index remains a useful backstop, not an authorization or shape check.
2. A narrowly scoped header admission guard should allow typed-marker inserts only through a dedicated, fixed-function owner identity that cannot be impersonated by application roles. It must also reject any direct or generic reversal referencing an existing typed original unless it came from the fixed reversal gateway, regardless of the new header's marker. This blocks both generic overloads and forged unmarked reversals for typed originals while leaving ordinary untyped ledger operations intact.
3. A narrowly scoped entry admission guard should look up its parent by globally unique transaction ID, not trust the child-supplied org, and prevent later direct additions to typed headers. The fixed gateway alone can create the exact initial legs; validate parent existence and org, property, currency, owner contact, signs, amounts, and absence of extraneous fields. Existing append-only behavior and existing RLS remain in force. Preexisting typed records must be classified against actual data, including zero/extra/malformed entries and generic reversals; ambiguous rows require quarantine or explicit remediation, never an assumed retroactive seal.
4. A `BEFORE INSERT` guard on `month_closes` can take the same org advisory lock even for legacy close workers. Typed gateways take that lock before checking the relevant closed period, so a close and typed operation are serialized. Exact lock namespace, transaction lifetime, snapshots after waits, isolation/retry behavior, lock order and rollback must be proven. This trigger affects close timing and compatibility, and must not alter ordinary untyped ledger behavior.
5. A separate narrowly scoped owner read gateway must return only the authorized individual's permitted typed transfer projection, with current session/org/environment and appropriate relationship checks. It must not expose other owners, unrelated ledger entries, payment instructions or banking details. Historical ownership and access rules need explicit design; a current property relationship does not automatically establish access to all history. Staff reads have their own role and scope.

The proposed admission identity must be a real, non-spoofable role boundary. A fixed-function `SECURITY DEFINER` owner could be considered only after checking that the app, authenticated, service and inherited roles cannot assume it, that it has only the necessary privileges, and that fixed function EXECUTE grants are narrow. Never rely on a caller-set JWT role, custom `SET LOCAL` capability, app flag, request field, or assumed role name as authority. In particular, a security-definer trigger itself changes `current_user`; a trigger that tests that changed identity can accidentally authorize everyone. Carefully design invoker-trigger identity and any minimal privileged parent lookup with pinned search paths and reviewed RLS behavior. Superuser, owners, disabled triggers and replication mode are operational trust boundaries to document, not protections proved by this draft. No blanket ledger writer fence, authenticated UPDATE grant, or universal migration framework is proposed.

**Compatibility is not automatic.** The current TypeScript writer directly inserts typed headers and entries and will be blocked by this seal. Codex must design and implement the fixed gateways and port the application, including safe mixed-version ordering and failure behavior; PE must validate the actual target, security and approved non-Mac application. Do not deploy the guards alone and assume old or current workers can keep writing typed records. No executable migration or complete function bodies exist yet; Codex still owns their concrete design, implementation, tests and independent review. PE owns target validation, security review, and separately approved non-Mac application. Existing typed rows and old-worker behavior must be inventoried before choosing rollout or quarantine.

## Separate B-014 source and reporting work

- Existing owner-payable entry contact is a recorded recipient identity, but not evidence of bank receipt or an immutable snapshot of the actual recipient of a tenant payment. `payments` has payer, status, amount and proof; lease payee details are instructions and can change. `src/server/statement-calculation.ts:27–55` selects confirmed payments by property/date and allocates invoice lines, but does not establish company-directed receipt. The source/recipient classification and its historical evidence must be designed before asserting company cash.
- Existing statement calculation derives closing as opening plus rent and other income less fee and expenses; `src/server/domain.ts:2357–2364` currently writes zero payouts. The full positive/zero/negative cross-period cash formula, actual typed payouts and reversals, deposit exclusion, and reconciliation must be designed and verified. `src/server/format.ts:81–100` includes an `owes` interpretation; B-014 scope requires negative net not imply owner repayment in screen or PDF.
- `owner_statements` already stores owner, period, opening, payouts, closing, issued actor/time and fee snapshot, and lines link to ledger/expenses. The period uniqueness and existing update policies do not themselves prove immutable corrected report revisions with reason, actor and time. Design exact version and source snapshot semantics before deciding the minimal missing persistence.
- Existing `notifications` and `deliveries` have related identifiers, recipient, channel, provider, status and timestamps (`20261018120000_schema.sql:1437–1491`). Examine whether they can safely bind a specific immutable report version and recipient and distinguish queued, sent, delivered and failed before proposing new metadata or tables. Delivery remains mocked locally and no external messages are authorized.
